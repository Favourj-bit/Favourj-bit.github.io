Hello, I’m Favour James. I am a Machine Learning Engineer and researcher from Nigeria, currently pursuing an MSc in Engineering Artificial Intelligence at Carnegie Mellon University Africa.

My research interests center on reliable and trustworthy AI. I am particularly interested in how AI systems can produce outputs that are grounded in evidence, remain robust under imperfect conditions, and can be evaluated to determine whether their reasoning and conclusions are genuinely supported. My interests include factuality and hallucination, evaluation and faithfulness, robustness and stress testing, structured knowledge and knowledge graphs, and reliable reasoning.

A question that motivates much of my work is: **How can structured knowledge and careful evaluation help AI systems reason more reliably, remain faithful to evidence, and recognize when available information is insufficient rather than producing unsupported answers?**

I participated in Google Summer of Code twice (2023 and 2024) under the National Resource for Network Biology, where I worked on [generating a PyTorch Geometric (PyG) graph dataset](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/archive/2023/projects/NBZn0Zm3) and [LLM-driven scientific extraction systems](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/archive/2024/projects/biCyAipp).

My 2024 GSoC project later developed into textToKnowledgeGraph, an LLM-based system for extracting molecular interactions from scientific literature, grounding biological entities, and representing the resulting information as structured biological statements and knowledge graphs. Working on this project strengthened my interest in grounding, factual faithfulness, structured representations, and the evaluation of AI-generated outputs.

I have also explored reliability from the perspective of robustness. I worked on evaluating how language models respond to typographical noise in Yorùbá question answering, which resulted in a publication at AfricaNLP, ACL 2025 and received the workshop’s Best Paper Award.

More broadly, I am interested in research at the intersection of AI and engineering, especially work that combines careful evaluation, structured information, and system building to develop AI systems that can be better understood, tested, and trusted in real-world settings.
